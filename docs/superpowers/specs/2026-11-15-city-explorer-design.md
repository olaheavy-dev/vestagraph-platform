# City Explorer: one page per council (design)

**Date:** 15 Nov 2026 · **Stage:** 3 (for the pilot, stage 5b) · **Decision:** [0055](../../decisions/0055-city-explorer-in-the-pilot.md) (PD-17) · **Research:** [City Explorer research](../../reviews/2026-11-14-city-explorer-research.md), [City Explorer data research](../../reviews/2026-11-15-city-explorer-data-research.md) · **Status:** approved by the founder (15 Nov 2026, prompt window: "Approve (Recommended)"), with the agent's calls marked in the text

## Intent
City Explorer gives people who are researching a move **before they have a postcode** a page for each council area in England. The founder chose it for the pilot to "Reach people earlier" (0055). In this brainstorm the founder set the bar: it must set Vestagraph apart from the ONS's free [Explore local statistics](https://www.ons.gov.uk/explore-local-statistics/) service and give people a reason to come back (founder, 15 Nov 2026, typed: "what would set us apart and keep customer hooked and also set us apart from ONS?").

The ONS service already shows 116 council indicators, compares councils ([City Explorer research](../../reviews/2026-11-14-city-explorer-research.md), 0055) and charts house prices by council ([data research](../../reviews/2026-11-15-city-explorer-data-research.md), topic 4). What it never does is turn a figure into **what a home would cost you each month**, or take you from a council **down to a postcode**. City Explorer is built around those two things.

## Goals
1. **Cost first.** The page opens with what living there costs: council tax for one band, the average monthly rent and the average house price, side by side.
2. **Rent or buy, per month.** The average rent beside the monthly cost of buying the average-priced home (repayment plus council tax), from the cost engine, with every assumption shown and the deposit, rate and term editable. These are council-level figures for anyone, not a saved home's costs: 0046 put a home's costs, compare and what changed behind sign-in, and the founder's choices here put their council-level forms on a public page; decision [0056](../../decisions/0056-no-on-page-chat-in-the-pilot.md) records that clarification of 0046 (its point 6) with the chat change.
3. **A chart for every topic**, each with a one-sentence plain-words caption built by rule from tested figures, never by AI.
4. **Your budget.** A monthly budget the visitor types; each cost line says whether it is within or above it.
5. **From council to postcode.** A "check a postcode in this council" box and a neighbourhood map whose areas open the postcode check.
6. **Reasons to return (part 2):** what changed since the last visit, compare two councils, council facts in the visitor's own ChatGPT or Claude (the area tools, MCP, with a link to the "Use in ChatGPT or Claude" page, 0056), share or print a summary.

## Non-goals
- Later topics (air quality, new homes, travel to work, universities) and dropped ones (broadband and mobile, life expectancy, earnings).
- Accounts, saved councils on a server, email alerts (0046: no accounts in the pilot).
- Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland: "not yet covered" (rule 6), though the data contract keeps a nation field.
- Price or rent forecasts of any kind.
- An on-page AI chat (moved to the signed-in workspace after the pilot, founder 15 Nov 2026).
- Searching by street or house: that is the postcode check's job.

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it looks good)
- **A ranking of councils**, a "1st of N", a table sorted by a figure, or a "best" or "better area" word (0054, FE-22, rule 4). Compare shows two councils side by side in the order the visitor picked them.
- **A score or rank drawn on a map** (0054: ranks are "never drawn on maps").
- **Advice.** Rent vs buy is arithmetic with its inputs on show, never "you should buy".
- **A figure typed by hand** (rule 2), a caption written by AI, or AI working out a number (rule 4, 0004).
- **Missing data shown as zero**, a flat line or a gap-free chart (rule 7): a missing month is a visible gap; a council a source doesn't publish (for example City of London for rents) says so.
- **A copy of the ONS service.** The page as a whole must add the cost and postcode links the ONS service lacks; if the design shows it only repeats that service, 0055's last consequence (narrow to its option A) applies. Single sections the founder chose (the affordability ratio, which the ONS also shows) stay; the average house price also feeds rent or buy and the budget check.

## Constraints
- **Unit:** one page per council, titled with its name (for example "Northbridge Council area"), searchable by city or town name (0055 point 3). Every figure is at its official level, with its source, edition and period (DA-14). Two-tier areas: almost every chosen source publishes at district level, so a page is for the district council; any figure published only for the county names the county (which figures, if any, is confirmed per source).
- **Look:** the pilot's brand system (soft gloss FE-29, ledger rows FE-30, source stamps FE-33, slate-ink dark mode FE-31, seven type sizes, three weights, `.design/prototype/tokens.css`). Charts are a new component and get their own style rules on the canvas's Brand and style page before the boards.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes; every chart has a table view and a text alternative (its caption plus the values); colour is never the only way a series is told apart; touch targets at least 44 px; no sideways scrolling at 320 to 390 px.
- **Charts:** values show on tap and on keyboard focus, never on hover only; provisional months are marked by a pattern as well as colour; a missing month reads "not published" in the table view; a changed figure is announced to screen readers; error messages are tied to their inputs; motion respects reduced motion; printed charts work in greyscale (B23).
- **Findable:** each council page has its own title and description and is listed in a sitemap, so people searching for a place find it (0055: "Reach people earlier"); how pages are rendered is settled with the stage 5b decisions (0048, FE-01).
- **Figures:** inputs live once in sample data with their source; every calculated figure (monthly amounts from the published yearly band amounts, the rent-vs-buy figures, caption changes, within or above budget, what changed) comes from tested code (rule 2), with test expectations worked out independently.
- **Privacy:** typed place names, postcodes, budgets, the rent-vs-buy inputs, the band picked and the compare choice are never stored or logged (0046, 0014, 0053). The budget and calculator inputs travel in the request body only, never the URL (a copied address would carry them); a postcode may use the URL fragment as 0046 allows; nothing in the query string. The privacy notice names them; the gate's log checks cover them (0053). A council page's own address (for example `/council/northbridge`) is an ordinary page view; a neighbourhood travels after the `#` and never reaches the logs. The council page view is logged like any page, with IP addresses dropped or anonymised before logs are written (0046); a council chosen from a place-name search therefore appears in that log as a page view, which is accepted, while the typed place name never does. Analytics (FE-05) never records form fields. Boundary files, map tiles, fonts and the chart library are served from our own hosting, so no third party sees a page view (B28). The new endpoints (budget, rent or buy, compare, place-name search) carry the same rate limits as the pilot's other public endpoints.
- **Licences:** every new source gets its DA-05 check and a `docs/references/data-sources.md` entry before build. "Bank of England" is a trade mark ([legal page](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/legal), as recorded in `data-sources.md`): the page names it as a source only.

## Approaches
Considered for the page (the founder chose B):
- **A. Same layout at council level** (the agent's recommendation): reuse the postcode result's rows, sheets and states, with trends only inside topic sheets. Fewest boards, smallest delay to the gate.
- **B. Chart-led profile page (chosen):** its own page, a chart for every topic, plus the features that set it apart. Distinctive; more boards, specs and sources; the gate moves later. The founder's reason is above. This replaces 0055 point 4's "reusing that layout" for the page; the brand system, source stamps, states, "How we got this" and About the data are still reused (noted on PD-17).
- **Dropped:** a council section inside the postcode result only (0055's option A, declined for "Reach people earlier").

Delivery (the founder chose the first): **two parts, both before the gate**; part 2 after launch; all at once.

## The page (part 1)
Sketch only. `<…>` marks a value the boards take from sample data or a generated file; the council is synthetic.
```
Northbridge Council area (sample)                [Compare] [Share]
Population <n> (mid-<year>)          Check a postcode here [________]

What it costs here
  Council tax, Band [D ▾] (example)  £<n> a year (£<n> a month)
  Average rent         £<n> a month
  Average price        £<n>
  Your monthly budget  [£____]   → each line: "within your budget" / "above it"

Rent or buy, per month                 Deposit [10%]  Rate [<r>%]  Term [25 yrs]
  Rent  ▇▇▇▇▇▇ £<n>    (rent £<n> + council tax £<n>)
  Buy   ▇▇▇▇▇▇▇▇ £<n>  (repayment £<n> + council tax £<n>)
  "On these inputs, buying the average-priced home costs £<n> more a month than the average rent."
  (or "… £<n> less …" / "… about the same as …"; averages of different homes)

Council tax by band      bars A–H                       + caption
Private rents            line, monthly                  + caption
House prices             line, monthly                  + caption
Affordability ratio      line, yearly                   + caption
Who rents, who owns      one stacked bar, 2021          + caption
Neighbourhoods           map + list; choose one → "See this neighbourhood" (its result page)
How we got this
```

### Sections in order (agent's order at the founder's request)
1. **Header:** the council's name, population as one line ("Population <n> (mid-<year> estimate)", ONS), the postcode box, and part 2's Compare and Share.
2. **What it costs here:** three figures side by side: council tax for the band picked, the average monthly rent and the average house price. A small band picker (A to H) starts at **Band D**, labelled "example" (founder: "Band picker, D default (Recommended)"; Band D is the official reference band every council publishes, while many homes are in lower bands (to cite from the VOA's council tax stock statistics at build), so the visitor can choose theirs). The band picked is used in the summary, rent or buy and the budget check; the generated file holds the example figures for all eight bands, so changing the band needs no request. Monthly council tax is the yearly amount divided by 12 (the rule #197 asks to record; this design relies on it). Council tax is the council area's average for a two-adult dwelling, parish and adult social care precepts included (MHCLG Table 9's note p, read 15 Nov 2026), and the label says so.
3. **Your budget:** one optional box, "Your monthly budget". Rent plus council tax and the buying cost plus council tax each say "within your budget" or "above it". Empty box: no labels, no nagging. *Agent's call:* handled like priority limits (0053): the amount goes to a tested module on the server, which answers within or above; never stored or logged; never in a share link or printout. The budget box keeps its value while the visitor moves between the council, neighbourhood and postcode pages within the same tab, held in the page's memory only: never in browser storage, so a reload or a new tab starts empty (no PECR question).
4. **Rent or buy, per month:** renting = the average monthly rent plus council tax for the band picked, monthly; buying = the monthly repayment on the average price (cost engine, `vestagraph_engine`) plus the same council tax. Council tax is on both sides because the person living in a home, owner or tenant, normally pays it (*agent's call*, fixing a builder-check P1: leaving it off the rent side would overstate the gap by the whole monthly council tax). With the example inputs the figures are in the generated file; when the visitor changes an input, the inputs go to the same tested module on the server (0053's pattern), never stored or logged. Example inputs, labelled "example" and editable: deposit 10%, term 25 years (*agent's calls*), and the Bank of England's published average rate on new mortgages for the latest month (founder). Labelled "repayment and council tax only; not the full cost of owning or renting", and "the average rent and the average price describe different homes" (the rent covers every size of rented home and the price every type sold), also in "How we got this". The difference sentence follows the caption rules, with three patterns: "more", "less" and "about the same" (within its own threshold in pounds, separate from "unchanged" for trends; see Plain-words captions). Inputs are checked first: deposit from 0% to below 100% of the price, a term of 1 to 40 years, a rate from 0% (repayment is then the loan divided by the months, no interest; worked test case needed) to a stated upper bound; negative, empty or non-numeric values say what is wrong and show no figure. **Rate (founder: "Rate for the deposit size (Recommended)", and "Both where each exists" for the product):** the Bank of England's published quoted rate for the deposit band that matches the deposit chosen (for example its rate for a 90% loan when the deposit is 10%), labelled with its month, band and product. The buy side is shown **per product**: the 2-year fixed at every band (60, 75, 85, 90, 95%) and the 5-year fixed where the Bank quotes one (75% and 95%); for any other deposit size the 5-year line reads "no published rate for this deposit size", never a rate borrowed from another band. The loan-to-value is 100% minus the deposit percentage, and the band used is the smallest published band at or above it (for example a 20% deposit, an 80% loan, uses the 85% band if the Bank publishes 75%, 85% and 90%), worked out in tested code with the Bank's band definitions cited (rules 2 and 3); a loan above the highest published band, or at or below 0, has no rate: it says so and asks for a typed rate. Bands mean "up to X% loan", so a small loan takes the lowest band that covers it (a 50% loan takes the 60% band). The bands, series codes and latest month (October 2026) are in the sources research; the series are the Bank's own, but the advertised rates come from Moneyfacts and no licence line is stated for them, so reuse is not confirmed (said in the sources table). **Missing inputs (rule 7):** if a council has no published rent or price (the data research lists City of London as not published for rents; to confirm at build), or the rate is unavailable, only the side that exists is drawn, with no difference sentence and no budget label for the missing side; the missing side says why, and for the rate asks the visitor to type one. **When an input changes:** the figures that depend on it are replaced by "Working out…" until the server answers; if the request fails or is refused (offline, rate limit), they read "Couldn't work this out" with Retry, never the old figure beside the new input; the newest request wins and older answers are dropped; typing is debounced so it stays under the rate limit. Each case gets a scenario.
5. **Council tax by band:** bars for A to H. The eight band amounts are **published inputs** from MHCLG's [council tax levels 2026-27](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/council-tax-levels-set-by-local-authorities-in-england-2026-to-2027) Table 9 ("Area council tax for a dwelling occupied by two adults by band"), recorded once per council with their source (founder: "Record all eight bands", reason "Match the publication"). There is **no band rule table and no tax rule table**: nothing calculated from Band D by proportions. A band's monthly amount is its published yearly amount divided by 12 in tested code (rule 2). Labelled "average for the council area, two adults, before discounts; parish charges vary". Table 9's own note gives the ratios of the other bands to Band D (6/9 to 18/9), and the statutory source is [Local Government Finance Act 1992, section 5(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/14/section/5) (checked in the design review, 15 Nov 2026); the tests may cite them as a cross-check, not as a rule the code applies.
6. **Private rents:** a monthly line from the ONS Price Index of Private Rents, with the latest provisional months marked "provisional" and the series' status quoted from the ONS at build. *Two ONS texts differ:* the bulletin says it became official statistics on 20 May 2026, "not yet accredited" (data research, topic 8), while the spreadsheet's cover sheet says "official statistics in development"; the licence check settles which to quote. Checked 15 Nov 2026 (recorded in the data research, topic 8): the [dataset](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/datasets/priceindexofprivaterentsukmonthlypricestatistics) spreadsheet (21 Oct 2026 edition, OGL v3.0) has a "Rental price" column in pounds per area, councils included, "rounded to the nearest £1" (its note 6), with index columns by bedroom count, from January 2015.
7. **House prices:** a monthly line from the UK House Price Index, newest 12 months marked "provisional". *Agent's call:* split by property type if the council download carries it (the research found that is what the ONS service lacks); otherwise one line.
8. **Affordability ratio:** a yearly line of the ONS house price to earnings ratio (workplace-based, as in the bulletin, or residence-based, as on the ONS service: chosen at the licence check and labelled), with breaks in method shown as breaks in the line, not joined.
9. **Who rents and who owns:** one stacked bar from Census 2021 tenure, labelled "Census day, 21 March 2021".
10. **Neighbourhoods:** the council outline split into neighbourhoods (LSOAs), on our own street map tiles (FE-03, still to price). Each is named with the readable name of the larger area it sits in plus its code, for example "Millbrook area (012C)" (a made-up name) (founder: "Larger area's name + code (Recommended)"; names from the House of Commons Library's list for the larger areas (MSOAs), licence to check; several neighbourhoods share a larger area's name, so the code stays). A list beside the map is the way in for keyboard and screen reader users (the shapes are not tab stops); choosing one opens a small panel with focus on its heading, Escape or the phone's Back closes it and returns focus. The panel shows the neighbourhood's name and "See this neighbourhood" (its result page, below). **The map is not coloured by any score** (0054: ranks are never drawn on maps). A line says what the map covers (B24). The council's own DfT connectivity figure appears in the page's facts; the scale direction is confirmed (research of 15 Nov 2026): a higher score is better connected, 0 to 100.
11. **How we got this:** one short section, one line per topic with its icon and source links, and a link to About the data (FE-24, FE-34 as amended). About the data gains City Explorer's sources, dates and limits.

### Neighbourhood result page (founder: "Neighbourhood result page")
For someone without a postcode, the bridge from council to street. **Its address carries the neighbourhood after the `#`** (for example `/council/northbridge#n=012C`), so the neighbourhood never reaches our logs, as with postcodes (0046; founder, 15 Nov 2026, prompt window: "Code after # (Recommended)", after the pre-push review found that an ordinary address would log which neighbourhood someone viewed). Neighbourhood pages are therefore not listed by search engines; council pages are. *Agent's calls, for the plan:* it reuses the postcode area result's layout (ledger rows, topic sheets and panels, states, "How we got this"), titled with the neighbourhood's name and code and "in <council>".
- **What it shows:** only facts published for that neighbourhood (LSOA) or a larger area, each labelled with its level (DA-14): for example DfT transport connectivity (LSOA), the deprivation rank and decile (0054, LSOA, with 0054's notes that it is relative, not absolute, and describes the neighbourhood, not a street), who rents and who owns (Census 2021, if published at that level); council-level facts are labelled with the council's name. Topics that need a postcode (nearby places and distances, flood by postcode, sold prices nearby) say "Check a postcode in this neighbourhood to see this", never worked out from the neighbourhood's centre.
- **Ways on:** a postcode box ("Have a postcode from a listing? Check it"), back to the council page, and the neighbourhood's place on the council map.
- **Never:** a picked postcode standing for the neighbourhood, a combined score, or a ranking of neighbourhoods (0054, rule 4).
- **Cost:** a new page with phone and desktop boards, light and dark, its states and its specification before the gate (0052).

### Plain-words captions
One sentence under each chart, built by a tested module from the generated figures, never by AI and never with a verdict word. Patterns (wording approved by the founder before build, like FE-39):
- trend: "Average rent rose £<n> (<p>%) in the 12 months to <month>." / "fell" / "was unchanged";
- single point: "In March 2021, <p>% of households rented privately.";
- provisional: "The latest <n> months are provisional and may change.";
- missing: "<Source> doesn't publish this for <council>." (rule 7).
"Unchanged" uses a stated threshold for trends, and "about the same" in rent or buy uses its own threshold in pounds (they measure different things); rounding follows the engine's rules (0021, 0036). No "steep", "sharp", "high" or "low".

### Search and the postcode link
- Search by council name, or by city or town name through OS Open Names settlements matched to councils by a tested name table (founder: "Use it, match by name (Recommended)"; the OS licence is the OGL, with the OS, Royal Mail and National Statistics attribution lines, refreshed quarterly; real data at stage 5b, sample places stay synthetic; sources research, row 12). Cases with scenarios: a place spanning several councils lists them all and the visitor picks (the sample data's synthetic place names two councils; for real data at 5b, OS Open Names gives **one** council per settlement, so listing every council a town spans needs a boundary-overlap check that OS Open Names alone does not support: an open 5b item); places sharing a name (for example Newport, Richmond) are listed with their county or region; "London" lists its councils; a county name lists its district councils; a place in Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland says "not yet covered"; an old council name (after a merger) leads to the current council with a line saying so.
- The postcode box checks the postcode belongs to this council; if not, it says which council it is in and still opens the result (*agent's call*).
- **The way back:** the postcode area result links to its council's page ("About <council>"), and the neighbourhood page links to both.

### States
Loading; council not found; place name matching several councils; a source missing for this council; a provisional or stale edition; all sources down; offline; Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland ("not yet covered"); two-tier figures at county level; a broken series (method change); the budget box with an invalid amount; rent or buy with an invalid deposit, rate or term (see section 4). Added after the design review: "Working out…" and "Couldn't work this out" for changed inputs; one side of rent or buy missing; no rate available; places sharing a name; London and county searches; an old council name; a series that starts at a council merger (no line drawn across the merger, a note saying when the council was formed); a council reorganised during the pilot (its page says it was replaced and links to the new one; "what changed" records for it are dropped with a note).

## Part 2
- **Compare two councils:** the visitor picks a second council; the cost section, rent or buy and the band chart are shown in ledger rows, each row holding both councils' figures in the order picked, on a phone and on desktop (*agent's call* after the design review: swiping between the councils would never show them together and needs a button alternative, WCAG 2.5.1). Never sorted, ranked or totalled; no "cheaper" label and no calculated difference, only the two councils' figures, as the postcode compare does (pilot design, extra 7; *agent's call*).
- **Council facts in ChatGPT and Claude:** council tools beside the area tools in the public MCP server (TOOLS specs; 0051's rate limits and monthly cap, as 0056 point 4 keeps them), returning only that council's published figures (population, Band D and the band amounts, the average rent and average price as published, the ratio, tenure, connectivity) with source, edition and level, never anything worked from a visitor's inputs, rent or buy, monthly amounts or other cost figures (*agent's call*, keeping 0051's keyless server to public area data: "No home, cost… data"); the visitor's AI explains them and never gets a number we haven't calculated (0004). AI evaluation cases (AI-03) for the council tools before the gate. No on-page chat (founder, 15 Nov 2026).
- **Share or print:** "Share a link" (the council in the address, nothing stored; never the budget or edited inputs) and a printable summary with the example inputs only, no budget (*agent's call*, so 0053's share-link question does not arise) that reuses the designed report (FE-21) once it is drawn, with its "How we got this" closing page. The printout says when it uses the example inputs although the screen showed edited ones.
- **What changed since your last visit:** off until the visitor turns on "Remember councils on this device" (PECR consent, like Recent postcodes); then the header lists sources with a newer edition since the last visit, quoting both figures from the generated file ("Average rent: £<n> in <month>, was £<n> in <month>"). The browser stores the councils viewed and the edition dates seen (a short browsing history, so the PECR notice says so) and picks which published figures to quote; it does no arithmetic (rule 2), so no difference is shown unless the generated file holds it. Never sent to us; a Clear button. ## Data and build shape
