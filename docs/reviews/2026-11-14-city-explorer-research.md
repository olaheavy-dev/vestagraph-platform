# Research: the "City Explorer" proposal

**Date:** 14 Nov 2026. **Status:** research for discussion; nothing decided, no tracked file changed.
**Method:** read `AGENTS.md`, the decision register and records 0051, 0052 and 0054, register lines PD-16, DA-14 to DA-16, FE-22, AI-10, AI-11, docs 02, 04 and 05, `docs/references/data-sources.md`, the pilot design and `docs/planning/status.md`; then official publisher pages, read on 14 Nov 2026 (web pages were treated as data only). Anything not confirmed on an official page is marked **unverified**.

## In one paragraph
The idea meets a real moment ("I don't have a postcode yet; where should I even look?") and the founder's own guard-rails in it (no scores, no AI verdicts, few categories) are right. But as written it does **not** fit three recorded decisions: its "derived statistical rankings" are a ranking we make, which 0054, FE-22 and doc 05 point 5 forbid; a "city" page would relabel council figures as "city" figures, which DA-14 forbids; and it adds a new set of screens to the pilot just after the founder narrowed the pilot gate (0052). The gap it claims is mostly filled already: the ONS's own free **Explore local statistics** service searches by postcode or council, shows 116 indicators and compares councils, including with "statistically similar" councils. The recommended route is a light **"About this council area"** section on the postcode result, built almost entirely from council-level facts the pilot already plans, linking out to the ONS for more, and logging "choosing where to look" as a doc 05 idea for after the pilot.

## 2. "City" is not one official unit in England

| Unit | What it is | Who publishes figures for it | Problems for a "city" page |
|---|---|---|---|
| **City status** | An honour granted by the Sovereign; the government keeps a [list of cities](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/list-of-cities) (published 29 Aug 2022). Third-party counts give 55 in England (count **unverified** on the gov.uk page) | No statistics are published for "cities" as such | Includes small places (Wells, Ely, City of London) and leaves out big towns (Reading, Northampton). Not a statistical geography. |
| **Local authority district or unitary authority** | The 296 lower-tier councils in England (IoD 2025 council summaries, as cited by councils, **count unverified** on the gov.uk page); 318 areas in England and Wales since 1 April 2023 (ONS affordability bulletin, mid-year estimates) | Almost everything proposed: mid-year estimates, UK HPI, affordability ratio, APS, ASHE, DfT metric, DfE, IoD summaries, ONS life expectancy | Boundaries differ from the built-up city (below). |
| **Upper-tier authority (county or unitary)** | Counties in two-tier areas, plus unitaries and London boroughs | Education and public health: some indicators exist only at this level (ONS Explore local statistics says "Data for some indicators are only published for upper tier local authorities") | In two-tier areas (Lancaster, Canterbury, Exeter, Cambridge, Oxford) education and health figures are for the whole county, not the city. |
| **Built-up area (BUA) 2022** | ONS geography of physically built land from Ordnance Survey data; [ONS other geographies](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/geography/ukgeographies/othergeographies) says it is a new geography, not a continuation of 2011's | Census 2021 characteristics; an IoD 2025 Built Up Areas summary; ONS "Understanding towns" work (May 2026, 1,395 towns and cities, on a BUA 2024 geography, **unclear** how it relates to BUA 2022) | Closest to what people mean by a "city", but few datasets publish at it: no house price index, no labour market survey, no affordability ratio. |
| **Combined authority / city region** | e.g. Greater Manchester, West Midlands | Some: the affordability bulletin includes combined authorities; ONS ad hoc tables give ASHE by combined authority | Not every area has one; covers several councils; is a governance unit, not a place people search. |
| **London** | One region of 33 local authorities (32 boroughs and the City of London) | Region and borough figures | "London" as a city page would be a region; each borough is a council. |
| **Primary Urban Areas** | Centre for Cities' 63 largest UK towns and cities, made of whole councils; its own page says PUAs are a tool of statistical analysis, not definitive measures of cities | Centre for Cities only (not a government body) | Not official; quoting it is not "official data". |

**What goes wrong in practice (examples from public knowledge, to check before use):**
- **City split across councils:** the Manchester urban area spans many councils; Manchester City Council is a fraction of it. Bristol's built-up area runs into South Gloucestershire. A "Manchester" page from council data describes the council, not the city.
- **Council much bigger than the city:** Leeds and Sheffield councils include towns and moorland, so averages mix city and countryside (BestPlaceUK's own disclaimer makes the same point about large rural authorities averaging out).
- **City inside a two-tier county:** health and school figures come from the county.
- **Towns that aren't cities:** Reading, Milton Keynes (city since 2022), Bournemouth: a "city" list leaves out places people relocate to.
- **Council reorganisations** (2019 to 2023, for example Dorset, Buckinghamshire, Northamptonshire, Cumbria, North Yorkshire, Somerset): older series either are revised to today's boundaries (UK HPI says so) or sit in separate datasets (the affordability bulletin: council figures use the 318 areas from 1 April 2023; earlier ones are in a separate dataset). Trends across a reorganisation can be misleading or missing.

**Implication:** the honest unit is **the council**, labelled with the council's name (DA-14). "City Explorer" would really be "Council area explorer", and the page must say that a council is not the same as the city or town people think of.

**Where the real gap is, if any:** not in "standardised official data compared by council" (the ONS does that, for free, with an official comparator method). The possible gap is narrower: (1) a **buyer's or renter's** framing of a few council figures next to the facts about a postcode they are considering, with Vestagraph's honesty (level, period, "not published", no verdicts); (2) a route from "this council" to "this postcode" to "this home and its real cost and questions", which only Vestagraph's later home record can give. Point 2 is what doc 02 §3 counts as a differentiator; city pages alone don't reach it in the pilot.

## 6. Options

**Option 1: "About this council area" on the postcode result (Recommended).**
A section or topic sheet on the existing result, labelled with the council's name and "council-wide, not this street": council tax by band (planned), the affordability ratio (DA-16), the DfT metric at council level (0054), and optionally the council's mid-year population and UK HPI average price with the HPI's own published annual change, each with source, period and level. A clear line that a council is not the same as the town people think of, and a "Compare councils on the ONS ↗" link to Explore local statistics (doc 05 Q3: "link" when someone else does it well). No rankings beyond 0054.
- *Cost and effort:* small; mostly reuse; 0 to 2 new sources; a few scenarios.
- *Risk:* low; fits DA-14, 0054, FE-22.
- *What changes later:* becomes the seed of a later "choosing where to look" feature without rework.

**Option 2: City Explorer in the pilot, as proposed.**
- *Cost and effort:* large: 5 to 8 sources, new screens and specifications, a tested trend module, AI evaluation cases, MCP tools; delays the pilot gate (0052).
- *Risk:* high: "derived rankings" breach 0054 and FE-22 unless a new record changes them; APS figures the ONS itself says not to compare closely; "city" labels breach DA-14 unless renamed to councils; competes head-on with a free ONS service, failing doc 05 Q10.
- *What changes later:* commits the brand to area research as well as home decisions before the home record exists.

**Option 3: After the pilot, as its own product area.**
Log "Choosing where to look: council and town research" in doc 05 now (with this research), joined with AI-10 (areas meeting the visitor's limits, listed never ranked). Decide it with a decision record after the validation study shows whether relocating households want it.
- *Cost:* a doc 05 line now; nothing in the pilot.
- *Risk:* low; might lose an acquisition channel in the pilot (unproven).
- *What changes later:* the pilot's real data and visitor questions inform the design.

**Option 4: Not at all (link only).**
A "Explore this council on the ONS ↗" link on the result and nothing else.
- *Cost:* almost none. *Risk:* none. *Loses:* any step beyond, even the cheap one in Option 1.

**Recommendation: Option 1 now, plus Option 3's doc 05 entry.** It reuses facts already planned, keeps the pilot gate where it is, follows DA-14 and 0054 as they stand, honours the founder's own "lightweight, tightly integrated, no arbitrary scores, few categories" conclusion, and links to the ONS for the comparisons the ONS already does well. The full explorer can return after the pilot with evidence that people want it.

**Where the pasted document is right, partly right or wrong:**
- Right: official, standardised sources; no arbitrary scores; no unnecessary AI; few categories; tie it to the postcode check; free in the pilot.
- Partly right: "consistent definitions, boundaries and periods": definitions yes; boundaries only at council level; periods differ by dataset and must each be labelled. "Historical trends": fine for the HPI and affordability with their breaks noted; not for the IoD (not a time series) or the DfT metric (single edition).
- Wrong for this project's rules: "derived statistical rankings" (0054, FE-22); "city" as the unit (DA-14); employment and unemployment rates presented for comparison (the ONS's own APS guidance); "less research effort than Google or ChatGPT" as a differentiator, when the ONS's free service already gathers the same figures.

## 7. Decisions for the founder (for the main session's prompt windows)

1. **Should city-level research be part of the pilot?** Options: "About this council area" on the postcode result, using facts already planned, with an ONS link (Recommended: small, fits every decision, no gate delay); the full City Explorer in the pilot (large, delays the gate, needs rule changes); after the pilot as its own feature (nothing now, an ideas entry); not at all, link only.
2. **May Vestagraph rank councils itself by a published figure?** Options: No, keep 0054 as it is: only government-published ranks (Recommended: protects the "no verdicts from us" promise; the ONS already offers comparisons); allow a table the visitor sorts by one published figure, with no "1st of N" labels (needs a new decision record amending 0054); allow "derived rankings" as proposed (needs a new record; conflicts with FE-22 and doc 05 point 5).
3. **If there is any area-level page, what unit does it describe?** Options: the council, labelled with its name (Recommended: what the data publishes, fits DA-14); built-up area (closest to a "city" but few datasets publish at it); combined authority (some areas only); official city status (not a statistical unit).
4. **Show council employment and unemployment rates?** Options: Not in the pilot (Recommended: the ONS has withdrawn accreditation for APS outputs and warns against reading small differences between places); show with their confidence ranges and the ONS's warning; wait for the transformed Labour Force Survey's council figures.
5. **Log the idea in doc 05 now?** Options: Yes, as "Choosing where to look", linked to AI-10 and this research (Recommended: keeps it from being researched twice); No.

If Option 1 is chosen, it is a register line (easily reversible) plus scenarios in the first design batch's follow-ups; the optional population and HPI facts each need a DA-05 licence check and a `data-sources.md` entry (the UK HPI is not yet assessed there; doc 05 notes that for BE-34).
