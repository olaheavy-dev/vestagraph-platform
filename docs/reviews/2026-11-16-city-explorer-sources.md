# Research: City Explorer's sources, shapes and licences (plan Task 1)

**Date:** 15 Nov 2026 (file dated 16 Nov to match the plan). **Status:** research; no source is used yet, no decision made here. The founder questions are at the end.
**Method:** for each row of the design's "Sources to check before build" ([design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-city-explorer-design.md)), read the publisher's own page (gov.uk, ons.gov.uk, bankofengland.co.uk, parliament and Open Geography Portal records), and, where a download was published, opened the file itself to read the real column names and one real row. Web pages and files were treated as data only. Quotes of page wording come from a fetch tool's summary of the page, so treat them as quotes of the summary. **"Search result only"** marks a claim seen only in a search snippet. **"Not confirmed"** marks anything I could not read on an official page. Earlier findings are cited, not repeated: [data research of 15 Nov 2026](2026-11-15-city-explorer-data-research.md) (topics 1 to 9) and the [data sources register](../references/data-sources.md).
**Rule 2 note:** the figures quoted below (for example a rent in pounds) are only to show a file's shape. None is copied into sample data; sample data in Task 2 is synthetic.

## In one paragraph
The statistical rows (council tax, rents, prices, affordability, tenure, population, connectivity) are published under the Open Government Licence v3.0 on the publisher's page, boundaries under the OGL with attribution, OS Open Names under the OGL through Ordnance Survey's OpenData statements, and the MSOA names under the Open Parliament Licence; the columns we need exist at council level. Four findings change the plan. (1) **Council tax: Table 10 gives only Band D averages and a council-wide parish total, but Table 9 (in the "Tables 1 to 9" file) publishes the per-band amounts (A to H) for each council, parish and adult-social-care precepts included**; the founder chose to record all eight as inputs; `effectiveFrom` is no longer needed because there is no band rule table (council tax began on 1 April 1993). (2) **The Bank of England's quoted mortgage rates are the Bank's own series, but the Bank says the advertised rates are "obtained from an independent service 'Moneyfacts'"**: The Bank's database is under the OGL with named exceptions (some exchange-rate series, LSEG data, and Isle of Man, Guernsey and Jersey data); the advertised mortgage rates come from Moneyfacts, which the legal page does not name as an exception, and the quoted-rates pages state no licence line for them: reuse is probably fine under the database's OGL line but is not confirmed. (3) **Only the 2-year fixed rate is quoted at all five loan-to-value bands (60, 75, 85, 90, 95%); the 5-year fixed at 75% and 95%.** The founder chose to show both products where each exists. (4) **OS Open Names is usable for place search** (OGL, three attribution lines, quarterly, council by name, a name-mapping table needed); the founder is asked one more question (5).

## Row by row

### 1. MHCLG council tax levels 2026-27 (Tables 9 and 10)
- **Source:** MHCLG, [Council Tax levels set by local authorities in England 2026 to 2027](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/council-tax-levels-set-by-local-authorities-in-england-2026-to-2027), published 25 March 2026; [technical notes](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/council-tax-levels-set-by-local-authorities-in-england-2026-to-2027/council-tax-levels-set-by-local-authorities-in-england-2026-to-2027-technical-notes). Files: Tables 1 to 9 (ODS) and "Table 10 Local Authority Level Data" (ODS, `Table_10_2026-27.ods`).
- **Licence (quoted):** "All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated".
- **Geography and level:** billing authorities in England (one row each, with the `ONS Code` GSS code in column B and an MHCLG `E-code` in column A), plus classes and England totals. The workbook's cover sheet says all 392 local authorities in England completed the form; the Table 10 sheet `Data_Billing` has 296 billing authorities with a GSS code, and these match the UK HPI council list except that the Isles of Scilly (E06000053) is in Table 10 and absent from the HPI property-type file (checked against both files, 15 Nov 2026).
- **Edition and cadence:** annual; 2026-27 edition; previous year's figures are in the same row (a few revised, marked `[r]`).
- **Fields to quote (sheet `Data_Billing`, header names as published):** `Authority`, `ONS Code`; "8. Average (Band D 2 Adult equivalent) council tax (including Adult Social Care precept and local precepts) (current year)" (the council's own Band D); "17. Average (Band D 2 adult equivalent) council tax for area of the billing authority including both local and major precepts" (the whole Band D bill, adding county, police, fire and combined authority; `[z]` where not applicable); "2. Parish precepts (current year)" (a total in pounds for the whole council); "18. Number of all parishes ..." and "18. Tax base of all parishes ...". Example of shape only: one district's row holds its own Band D and, in column 17, a larger all-precepts Band D.
- **Per-band amounts: not in Table 10 (no band columns), but published in Table 9 of the "Tables 1 to 9" file** (`Tables_1-9_2026-27.ods`, sheet `Table_9`, "Area council tax for a dwelling occupied by two adults by band 2026 to 2027"; columns `E Code`, `ONS Code`, `Authority`, `Region`, `Class`, `Area`, `Band A` to `Band H`; 296 council rows with a GSS code (counted), the same count as Table 10's `Data_Billing`; same licence and publication as above). Its notes: "Figures include parish precepts and adult social care precepts." and "The ratios of other bands to Band D are: A 6/9; B 7/9; C 8/9; E 11/9; F 13/9; G 15/9; H 18/9." (note ac); the technical notes say dwellings "pay a proportion of the Band D council tax set for the authority" (section 5.11). I checked one row by hand: a district's Band A equals its Band D times 6/9 to the penny, and its Band D equals Table 10's column 17 for that council. So the engine may derive bands from Band D (design; Local Government Finance Act 1992 section 5(1), verified by the earlier design review) and the tests can cite Table 9 as independent published figures. This corrects the earlier register note's "by its title" reading: it is now confirmed by reading the file.
- **Parish precepts:** inside the Band D figure (technical notes: the requirement used for the average is "including adult social care and parish precepts"), and Table 10 gives only a council-wide total, not per-parish amounts. A home in a parish with a different precept than the council average cannot be found from this table: say so in the product (rule 7), do not show the average as that home's bill.
- **Start of council tax:** the technical notes, section 5.10: "It replaced the community charge on 1 April 1993." So the band table's `effectiveFrom` is **1 April 1993** (England).
- **Name caveat:** Band D here is "2 Adult equivalent", so the wording must not imply every household pays it.
- **Pending approval:** Table 8c note x says one authority (Lewes District Council) had its 2026-27 average "pending approval subject to a full council decision on 26 March 2026"; the value could differ from what was finally set (not confirmed).
- **Not confirmed:** the Welsh equivalent (out of scope, England first, rule 6); revisions after 25 March 2026.

### 2. ONS Price Index of Private Rents (PIPR)
- **Source:** ONS dataset [Price Index of Private Rents, UK: monthly price statistics](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/datasets/priceindexofprivaterentsukmonthlypricestatistics); the file read is the 21 October 2026 edition.
- **Licence (quoted):** "All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0" (the page's footer adds "except where otherwise stated"); the workbook cover adds "Crown copyright © 2026".
- **Edition and cadence:** monthly; the 21 Oct 2026 edition runs to September 2026; next release 18 November 2026 (page). Index base January 2023 = 100; series from January 2015.
- **Status wording:** the workbook cover says "These series are official statistics in development." (The earlier research recorded "not yet accredited"; the file's own words are the ones to quote.)
- **Geography and level:** one sheet, `Table 1`, long format: UK, countries, English regions, and local areas. For September 2026 the file has 294 English councils (164 E07, 62 E06, 36 E08, 32 E09), 22 Welsh, 18 Scottish (S33), 9 English regions (E12), and Northern Ireland areas with no value. The City of London is not among the E09 rows (32 of 33), and the Isles of Scilly could not be found in the E06 rows: so **some councils have no published rent**, which the design's "council with no published rent" sample case matches.
- **Fields to quote (Table 1):** `Time period` (an Excel date serial, so convert), `Area code`, `Area name`, `Region or country name`, `Index`, `Monthly change`, `Annual change`, `Rental price` (whole pounds per month, rounded to £1, note 6), and the same four columns for one, two, three and four-or-more bedrooms, and for detached, semidetached, terraced and flat or maisonette.
- **Which status to quote:** the file has no tenancy-status or provisional column; there is one `Rental price` series (new and existing tenancies, cover sheet). Note 4: `[x]` = not available, note 5: `[z]` = not applicable: both must be read as missing, never as zero (rule 7).
- **Not confirmed:** whether any recent month is later revised (the page does not say; the bulletin recommends longer-term trends: earlier research).

### 3. UK House Price Index
- **Source:** HM Land Registry with ONS, [UK House Price Index: data downloads August 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/uk-house-price-index-data-downloads-august-2026--2) (the latest edition on the [reports collection](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/uk-house-price-index-reports), published 21 October 2026); republishing terms in [About the UK House Price Index](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/about-the-uk-house-price-index/about-the-uk-house-price-index).
- **Licence (quoted):** "All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated". **Attribution required** (About page, "Republishing data"): "Contains HM Land Registry data © Crown copyright and database right 2020. This data is licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0." and "include information about the nature of the data and any relevant dates for the period of time covered". The page also says the UK HPI is a National Statistic.
- **Edition and cadence:** monthly; the files are named for the data month (`...-2026-08.csv`); each month adds a new month and revises the previous 12.
- **Property type by council: yes.** `Average-prices-Property-Type-2026-08.csv` has `Date`, `Region_Name`, `Area_Code` and, for detached, semi-detached, terraced and flat: `..._Average_Price`, `..._Index`, `..._Monthly_Change`, `..._Annual_Change`. For August 2026 it holds 62 E06, 164 E07, 36 E08 and 33 E09 rows, so every English council except the Isles of Scilly. **Gaps are real:** the City of London row has empty detached, semi-detached and terraced cells (flats only), so a blank is a gap, not zero.
- **All-types average and sales volume:** `UK-HPI-full-file-2026-08.csv` has `AveragePrice`, `Index`, `1m%Change`, `12m%Change` and `SalesVolume` among other columns (header read; the full-file row check for one council was not repeated).
- **Not confirmed:** the licence line for the Land Registry's separate search tool beyond the same page footer.

### 4. ONS housing affordability ratio
- **Source:** ONS, [Housing affordability in England and Wales: 2025](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/housing/bulletins/housingaffordabilityinenglandandwales/2025), released 26 March 2026, next March 2027.
- **Licence (quoted):** "All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated".
- **Basis:** the bulletin's glossary says its analysis "uses earnings data based on the place of work", so the headline ratio is **workplace-based**. A **residence-based** series is also published as a separate dataset, and the ONS Explore indicator "Housing affordability ratio (residence-based)" (earlier research). Which one the product quotes must be named on the chart (a person's own pay is not the workplace's pay).
- **Datasets and tables (workbook opened):** [House price to workplace-based earnings ratio](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/housing/datasets/ratioofhousepricetoworkplacebasedearningslowerquartileandmedian) (release 26 March 2026, next March 2027, OGL v3.0), file `aff1ratioofhousepricetoworkplacebasedearnings.xlsx`. Council level is **Table 5c ("Median affordability ratio")**: "Ratio of median house price to median gross annual workplace-based earnings by local authority district, England and Wales, 1997 to 2025", with 5a (median house price, "year ending September 1997 to 2025 (£)") and 5b (median earnings). Lower-quartile versions are 6a to 6c; countries and regions 1a to 1c; counties 3a to 3c. The residence-based dataset ([page](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/housing/datasets/ratioofhousepricetoresidencebasedearningslowerquartileandmedian), file `aff2ratioofhousepricetoresidencebasedearnings.xlsx`) has the same release date and licence line; its workbook was not opened. Former local authorities and combined authorities are separate datasets.
- **Reuse wording in the workbook:** "These statistics were adapted from data from the Land Registry licensed under the Open Government Licence v.3.0. Users should include a source accreditation to ONS - Source: Office for National Statistics."
- **Accreditation:** not stated on the bulletin or dataset pages (not confirmed).
- **Not confirmed:** column layout within sheet 5c (the sheet itself was not read); the residence-based workbook.

### 5. Census 2021 tenure (TS054)
- **Source:** ONS dataset [TS054 Tenure](https://www.ons.gov.uk/datasets/TS054/editions/2021/versions/3) (the ONS dataset service API lists versions 1 to 4; version 4 of 28 March 2023 is the newest) and the Nomis [bulk download](https://www.nomisweb.co.uk/sources/census_2021_bulk) `census2021-ts054.zip`.
- **Licence (quoted):** "Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated" (ONS dataset page). The Nomis bulk page shows no licence wording (only a "Crown Copyright" link): not confirmed there.
- **Council level:** the ONS dataset service gives lower-tier local authorities (and filters to larger areas). **Small-area level:** the Nomis zip, opened, holds `census2021-ts054-lsoa.csv`, `-msoa.csv`, `-oa.csv`, `-ltla.csv`, `-utla.csv`, `-rgn.csv`, `-ctry.csv` and a metadata file.
- **Fields (both LSOA and LTLA files):** `date`, `geography`, `geography code`, then counts of households: "Tenure of household: Total: All households", "Owned", "Owned: Owns outright", "Owned: Owns with a mortgage or loan", "Shared ownership", "Social rented", "Social rented: Rents from council or Local Authority", "Social rented: Other social rented", "Private rented", "Private rented: Private landlord or letting agency", "Private rented: Other private rented", "Lives rent free". The ONS page warns people mix up landlord types and says to add the two social rented categories together (and the two private).
- **Geography caveat:** these are 2021 geographies. Councils that merged after 2021 will not match 2026 council codes (20 of 309 England LTLA codes in the file are not among the August 2026 HPI council codes, and 6 HPI codes are not in the file; the reasons were not checked). Matching needs a published change record: **not confirmed**.
- **Not confirmed:** the Nomis licence line; response-rate notes beyond the earlier research.

### 6. ONS mid-year population
- **Source:** ONS, [Population estimates for England and Wales: mid-2025](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/populationestimates/bulletins/populationestimatesforenglandandwales/mid2025), released 29 July 2026; next release to be announced.
- **Licence (quoted):** "All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated".
- **Status:** "accredited official statistics" (page: "These accredited official statistics were independently reviewed by the Office for Statistics Regulation in March 2017").
- **Level:** 318 local authority areas in England and Wales; the page says "The size of the population increased in 75% of all local authorities in the year to mid-2025 (240 out of 318 areas)."
- **The "official estimates" plan has changed:** the page says "In our previous Population estimates for England and Wales: mid-2024 bulletin, we stated our intention for modelled admin-based population estimates (ABPEs) to become the official estimates of the population in 2026. This is no longer our aim." So the mid-year estimates stay the official series; my first note's wording on this was wrong and is withdrawn.
- **Table and column for council population (file opened):** dataset [Estimates of the population for England and Wales](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/populationestimates/datasets/estimatesofthepopulationforenglandandwales), workbook `mye25tablesew.xlsx` ("Mid-Year Population Estimates, England and Wales, June 2025", published 9.30am 29 July 2026; "The next publication is scheduled for summer 2027."). The council figure is in sheet **`MYE4`** ("Population estimates: Summary for England and Wales, mid-2011 to mid-2025"): columns `Code`, `Name`, `Geography`, `Mid-2025`, `Mid-2024`, `Mid-2023` and so on back to `Mid-2011`, one row per area (296 E06 to E09 council codes read). Age and sex detail is in `MYE2 - Persons`.
- **Boundary basis (workbook Notes sheet):** "where subnational level data are presented, the population estimates reflect boundaries in place as of April 2024" (note 4), while note 5 says the file "uses boundaries for England and Wales that existed at publication, which includes the changes made in April 2023". The two notes are worded differently, so which boundary date applies is **not confirmed** beyond "April 2023 to April 2024"; the header line must name the edition and must match councils by code.
- **Not confirmed:** the licence line inside the workbook (the bulletin's OGL line is quoted above).

### 7. DfT transport connectivity metric
- **Source:** Department for Transport, [Transport connectivity metric](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/transport-connectivity-metric/transport-connectivity-metric), published 29 September 2025; file `connectivity_metrics_2025.ods` (read in full for structure).
- **Direction of the scale: confirmed, higher is better.** Page: the metric "will provide a score in a range from 0 to 100 where 100 represents the most connected area"; the file's metadata: "connectivity scores range from 0 to 100, where 100 represents the highest level of connectivity" and "Scores represent the ease of access to services, with higher scores indicating better connectivity". The file also defines deciles ("1 represents the lowest connectivity, 10 represents the highest"). Scores are relative: "Scores should be interpreted relative to other areas" and "Different transport modes should not be directly compared".
- **Licence:** the page says "This publication is licensed under the terms of the Open Government Licence v3.0 except where otherwise stated". **But the file's own metadata row says `license: TBA`.** Treat the page wording as the licence and record the mismatch.
- **Status:** the page says "All results presented in this publication are provisional results for 2021 LSOA boundaries and 2021 data sources"; the file's metadata says "statistical designation: Experimental Statistics", version `V1.0.0`, `publication_frequency: Annual (tentative)`, `next update: TBD`, time period "Q4 (October to December 2024)".
- **Sheets and columns:** sheets `Metadata`, `OA`, `LSOA`, `LAD`, `RGN`. `LSOA` is keyed by `LSOA21CD`; `LAD` is keyed by **`LAD22NM` (a 2022 council name, no code)**, so linking a council needs a name match to a 2022 name: a risk for renamed or merged councils (not checked which). Score columns (0 to 100): per purpose and mode, for example "Employment (walking)", "Overall (walking)", "Overall (cycling)", "Overall (public transport)" (its first column reads "Business (public transport)", not Employment), "Overall (driving)", "Employment (overall)" to "Residential (overall)", and **`Overall`** as the last column (the all-modes, all-purposes score). Recommended quote for 0054: `Overall` at LSOA and LAD (name-matched).
- **Not confirmed:** the decile columns' sheet and names (the metadata defines deciles but the sheets read show only score columns).

### 8. Bank of England quoted mortgage rates by loan-to-value band
- **Source:** Bank of England, [Quoted household interest rates (visual summary)](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/visual-summaries/quoted-household-interest-rates) (last updated 27 May 2026) and [Further details about quoted household interest rates data](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/details/further-details-about-quoted-household-interest-rates-data). Series descriptions and the latest values were read from the Bank's own Statistical Interactive Database (the CSV export of the series).
- **Latest month:** **October 2026** (value dated 31 Oct 2026; the series carry monthly averages, published "on the 5th working day of the following month", not seasonally adjusted).
- **Loan-to-value bands and series codes (fixed-rate mortgages to households, "UK monetary financial institutions (excl. Central Bank) sterling", percent):**

| Product | 60% | 75% | 85% | 90% | 95% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2-year fixed | `IUMZICQ` | `IUMBV34` | `IUMZICR` | `IUMB482` | `IUM2WTL` |
| 3-year fixed | not found | `IUMBV37` | not found | not found | not found |
| 5-year fixed | not found | `IUMBV42` | not found | not found | `IUM5WTL` |
| 10-year fixed | not found | `IUMBV45` | not found | not found | not found |

  The Bank also quotes 2-year variable at 75% (`IUMBV48`), 90% (`IUMB479`) and 95% (`IUM2WDT`), a lifetime tracker (`IUMBV24`), a revert-to rate (`IUMTLMV`) and a buy-to-let 2-year fixed at 75% (`IUMZID4`). **The products come from the series names in the Bank's database**, not from either Bank page: for example `IUMZICQ` is described there as "Monthly interest rate of UK monetary financial institutions (excl. Central Bank) sterling 2 year (60% LTV) fixed rate mortgage to households (in percent) not seasonally adjusted", and likewise `IUMZICR` (2 year, 85% LTV), `IUMB482` (2 year, 90%), `IUM2WTL` (2 year, 95%), `IUMBV42` (5 year, 75%) and `IUM5WTL` (5 year, 95%). "Not found" means not among the 26 series codes of the Bank's "Quoted Rates" view (a search-result link; I read the descriptions of the 15 mortgage-rate codes in it); I did not confirm that no other term and band combination exists elsewhere in the database. The further-details page says rates are selected for "the lowest available for loans of 60%, 75%, 85%, 90% and 95% LTV respectively" and are "generally" for loans "in the range of £25,000 to £500,000".
- **Latest values:** each series has a value for October 2026 (the 2-year 75% and 5-year 75% series differ by a few tenths of a point); the numbers belong in the Bank feed, not in this note (rule 2).
- **Are they the Bank's own data? Partly: the series are the Bank's, the advertised rates come from Moneyfacts.** The Bank's methodology page: rates are "obtained from an independent service 'Moneyfacts'" and "weighted together using business volumes reported by those institutions". The Bank's [legal page](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/legal) says the database is "the copyright of the Governor and Company of the Bank, unless otherwise stated" and "Reproduction of data in the Database is subject to the terms of the UK Open Government Licence", with named exceptions: "selected exchange rate data and series", LSEG data ("we do not in fact own the copyright to that data, LSEG do") and Isle of Man, Guernsey and Jersey data. **Moneyfacts is not named as an exception, and the quoted-rates pages state no licence or third-party terms.** So the series are not LSEG data, but whether Moneyfacts keeps rights in the underlying advertised rates is **not confirmed**. Wording used in every file: "The Bank's database is under the OGL with named exceptions (some exchange-rate series, LSEG data, and Isle of Man, Guernsey and Jersey data); the advertised mortgage rates come from Moneyfacts, which the legal page does not name as an exception, and the quoted-rates pages state no licence line for them: reuse is probably fine under the database's OGL line but is not confirmed." Suggested handling: attribute "Source: Bank of England" with the OGL line and the month, and ask the Bank's statistics enquiries to confirm before the live feed (stage 5b).
- **Mapping a deposit to a band:** not decided here: see Questions.

### 12. Place-name-to-council lookup (0055)
The founder chose "Check OS Open Names first" (see Questions, 3), so it was read closely: the Ordnance Survey documentation, the OS OpenData licence statements, and the October 2026 download itself (opened).

- **ONS Index of Place Names in Great Britain (IPN).** Latest edition on the portal: July 2024 (a July 2025 edition was not found). Every portal item's licence field points to the ONS [geography licences page](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/geography/licences); mirrors show "No Licence Provided" and "Not set", and older records say the data holds both OS and ONS intellectual property rights (search result only). This is the **licence conflict the design review found**, still unresolved by an official statement. Not recommended.
- **OS Open Names: licence.** The OS "OpenData questions and answers" on the [OS product support page](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/products/product-support) says: "OS OpenData is licensed under the Open Government Licence, which allows you to use the OS OpenData datasets in any way and for any purpose. We simply ask that you acknowledge us with "Contains OS data © Crown Copyright [and database right] (year)"." and "OS OpenData is free to use under the Open Government Licence (OGL)". The Ordnance Survey copyright-acknowledgments page says the same ("We use the Open Government Licence (OGL) for OS OpenData") and: "The same acknowledgement should be included in any sub-licences of the data that you grant, including any further re-use." The product's own pages do not repeat the licence; the Open Names documentation calls it "a comprehensive open dataset" and the download is served without a key from OS's download API. The download's own `Doc/licence.txt` says: "Your use of OS OpenData is subject to the terms at http://os.uk/opendata/licence." That address redirects to the National Archives' [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/), which permits you to "exploit the Information commercially and non-commercially for example, by combining it with other Information, or by including it in your own product or application" if you "acknowledge the source of the Information ... by including or linking to any attribution statement specified by the Information Provider(s)". So the licence is the OGL v3.0 (read on the OGL page), reached through the OS statements and the download's licence file, not through a licence line on the product page.
- **Attribution it needs:** the download's `licence.txt` lists three statements: "Contains OS data © Crown Copyright and database rights 2026", "Contains Royal Mail data © Royal Mail copyright and database right 2026" and "Contains National Statistics data © Crown copyright and database right 2026". OS's support page requires the second and third only "if you use Code-Point Open data"; Open Names includes postcode records, and its file lists all three. **Safest reading: carry all three lines** wherever place results appear (About the data page and, as the OS guidance asks for websites, an acknowledgement where the data is used); using only the settlement rows may need only the first (not confirmed: OS does not say so).
- **Cadence and edition:** "This product is updated quarterly in January, April, July, October." The file read is the October 2026 edition (files dated 15 October 2026), 824 files, CSV zip 103 MB (GB), plus GML and GeoPackage.
- **Does it map a place to a council? Yes, by name.** `Doc/OS_Open_Names_Header.csv` gives 34 columns including `NAME1`, `TYPE`, `LOCAL_TYPE`, `GEOMETRY_X`, `GEOMETRY_Y`, `POPULATED_PLACE`, `DISTRICT_BOROUGH`, `COUNTY_UNITARY`, `REGION` and `COUNTRY` (each with a URI). The documentation: "The points are queried against Boundary-Line to populate the contextual geography attributes such as DISTRICT_BOROUGH or COUNTY_UNITARY." Settlements are `TYPE` `populatedPlace` with `LOCAL_TYPE` City, Town, Village, Hamlet, Suburban Area or Other Settlement: 43,274 rows in GB; 33,426 in England, and every English settlement row had a `DISTRICT_BOROUGH` or `COUNTY_UNITARY` (for a unitary the district field is empty and the unitary's name is in `COUNTY_UNITARY`). Same-name places are separate rows with their own council and region, for example "Clapham" in Lambeth (Greater London), Arun (West Sussex), Teignbridge (Devon), Bedford and North Yorkshire, and "Newbury" in West Berkshire, Somerset and Wiltshire, so "choose, labelled with its county" works from the data.
- **Two limits found in the file:** (1) it names the council and gives a URI, but **no GSS council code**, so each row must be joined to our council records by name. In my test against the UK HPI council names, 1,375 of the 33,426 English settlement rows (4%) carry a council name that does not match exactly (for example "County of Herefordshire", "City of Stoke-on-Trent", "St. Helens", "St. Albans", "King's Lynn and West Norfolk", "Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole", "The City of Brighton and Hove"), so a small hand-kept name-mapping table, checked by a test, is needed. A spatial join (the place's `GEOMETRY_X`, `GEOMETRY_Y` point inside a council boundary) would give codes but needs the boundaries loaded (row 9) and a point-in-polygon step. (2) The council names follow OS's Boundary-Line edition; the edition is not stated in the files I read, so a council merger could lag by a release (not confirmed).
- **The councils' own names** from the boundary layer (`LAD26NM`) plus the council names and codes in the other files: OGL (row 9), enough for council-name search with no new source.
- **Recommendation: use OS Open Names settlements (not roads or postcodes), joined to councils by name through a tested mapping table, with all three attribution lines carried.** It is the only candidate whose licence is readable on official pages, it covers about 33,000 English places with a council field, and it keeps rule 1 (no invented place-to-council facts): the council comes from OS's data, not from us. Cost: a new data source, an attribution block, a name-mapping table to keep, and a quarterly refresh. Question 5 asks the founder.
